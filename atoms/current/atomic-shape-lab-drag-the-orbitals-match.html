6 — teaching notes

1. One line, two shapes. The D-line wavelength is the energy gap between the s and p states. Because both states share the same n, the gap is fixed by the difference in effective charge. The student can slide either shape and watch the line move — the line is a joint constraint.

2. Penetration is the difference between the shapes. Zp < Zs for every atom, so the p electron feels a weaker core: it penetrates more and its cloud is larger on average, not smaller. The cloud panel shows this directly.

3. Sensitivity is the point. A 1% error in the energy gap is a 1% error in wavelength; at 589 nm that is 6 nm, which is far wider than the line. The spectrum is a sharp instrument — which is why our binding-channel constants could not predict these lines to better than ~8 nm.

4. The discovery waiting here. In the table below, δs−δp is nearly the same for Na, K, Rb and Cs — and lithium is conspicuously different. That is the same lithium deviation found twice earlier, in two unrelated parameterizations, from two unrelated measurements. Ask the student to find it.

5. What this model is not. These are effective one-electron shapes: hydrogenic forms rescaled by an effective charge. Real atoms have correlated motion, and the collective terms we measured (a completed-shell excess of +0.033 per partner, a half-filled exchange residual) are exactly the physics this picture cannot hold. The lab shows the shape that the spectrum implies; it does not claim it is the whole wavefunction.

7 — honest caveats

Effective defect ≠ spectroscopic quantum defect. Here δ = n(1 − 1/Zeff) is a re-parameterization of our own effective charge, not the tabulated quantum defect from NIST/CRC. For inner shells the two coincide; for a valence electron they do not, because the valence electron sees a screened nucleus rather than a bare one. Use this lab for shapes and intuition, not for citing quantum-defect tables.

δp inherits a second assumption. Zp comes from the D-line gap, not from a binding energy, so the p "shape" is fixed by the transition rather than measured independently. That is exactly what makes the matching exercise work, and also why a mismatch would blame the model and the assumption together.

Channel scope. This lab covers position and shape. Intensity (radial overlap strengths) uses the radial functions computed here but is not tested against measured oscillator strengths — that check is still open in the Strength Channel Probe.
