## 1. Handedness and Parity: The Fundamental Neutrino Structure

In the SRC framework, the distinction between a neutrino ($\nu$) and an antineutrino ($\bar{\nu}$) is purely mechanical:

* **Symmetric Vortices:** A neutrino and an antineutrino are the same fundamental topological vortex, differentiated exclusively by their relative sense of rotation (chirality/handedness).
* **Kinematic Alignment:** 
  * Left-handed vorticity (relative to translation vector $\mathbf{v}$) defines the $\nu$ state.
  * Right-handed vorticity (relative to translation vector $\mathbf{v}$) defines the $\bar{\nu}$ state.
* **Mutual Induction:** When counter-rotating $\nu$ and $\bar{\nu}$ vortices encounter one another, the velocity field of each vortex acts on the other. Because their circulation vectors are opposing, they mutually induce a co-linear translational velocity. This minimizes local shear strain on the surrounding superfluid lattice, mechanically locking them into an aligned, counter-rotating, neutral propagating pair: the photon.

## 2. Long-Range Gravity as Vortex Filaments (Aetheric Chains)

Gravity does not operate via unshielded massless gauge bosons or abstract geometric curvature of empty space. It is the macroscopic tensile network of the condensate:

* **Filament Formation:** Under large-scale rotational shear and mass displacement (galaxies, stellar systems), uncoupled $\nu/\bar{\nu}$ components within the condensate spontaneously align along lines of least action, forming continuous vortex filaments or "quantum strings."
* **Tensile Coupling:** These vortex chains act as macroscopic tensile lines spanning between baryonic anchor points. What is classically measured as gravitational attraction is the mechanical tension of these stretched filaments resisting spatial divergence.
* **Scale Invariance:** The same fluid pressure gradient that enforces $\nu-\bar{\nu}$ pairing at the microscopic scale drives long-range filament alignment across galactic scales, dictating flat outer rotation profiles without invocation of dark matter.

## 3. Light Propagation and the Speed of Light ($c$)

Light is not an independent projectile traversing a vacuum void; it is an acoustic/shear excitation of the condensate lattice:

* **Propagation Mechanism:** A photon is a localized, transverse Goldstone shear wave travelling along the pre-stressed lattice formed by the $\nu-\bar{\nu}$ condensate.
* **Physical Origin of $c$:** The speed of light is the characteristic shear-wave propagation velocity ($c_s$) of the aetheric substrate, governed by its bulk modulus ($K$) and ground-state density ($\rho_s$):
  $$c = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho_s}}$$
* **Refraction and Lensing:** Variations in local mass concentration induce density gradients ($\nabla \rho_s$) and filament tension within the medium. Light bends near massive bodies because it traverses a medium of variable density and acoustic impedance, eliminating the need for geometric space contraction.

## 4. Interaction Kinematics: Scattering and Force Transfer

Classical "wave-particle" anomalies are resolved through continuous fluid mechanics when light encounters matter or aligned filaments:

* **Gear-Mesh Coupling:** Because a photon carries intrinsic rotational torque ($\omega$), striking an aligned filament or atomic boundary layer constitutes an immediate physical interface. The interaction is analogous to rotating gears meshing.
* **Momentum Transfer (Radiation Pressure):** Force transfer occurs via direct kinetic shear: the translational momentum and rotational energy of the propagating vortex are imparted to the encountering lattice structure.
* **Scattering and Absorption:** When a photon encounters an out-of-phase or densely packed filament network that cannot transmit its specific frequency, the vortex sheds its rotational torque into the local medium (thermal/lattice excitation) or deflects along the boundary layer. Energy exchange is deterministic fluid contact, replacing abstract probability amplitudes with rotational torque and acoustic coupling.
